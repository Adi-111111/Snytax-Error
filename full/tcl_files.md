# How the tcl files work

I started from Ed Stott's `maths-accelerator` base overlay, which is a generic PYNQ-Z1 reference design with HDMI-in capture and HDMI-out display both wired up, plus a placeholder IP slot. That's not what I need — I just want my `pixel_generator` IP (the MLP forward-pass pipeline) feeding video straight to the monitor, with nothing coming in.

I could do that rewiring by hand in the Vivado GUI, and the first couple of times I did. The problem is every time I change something in the SystemVerilog — resize H1/H2, fix a port, whatever — I have to rebuild the whole block design from scratch, and doing the same sequence of GUI clicks perfectly every single time is exactly the kind of thing I get wrong sometimes. So I wrote three TCL scripts to do it instead. They run in this order: `repackage_pixel_generator.tcl`, then Ed Stott's own `base.tcl`, then my `patch_base_to_lean_nn.tcl`. `build_lean_nn_bitstream.tcl` just chains all three together with synthesis and bitstream generation so I only have to run one command.

## repackage_pixel_generator.tcl

This turns my six `.sv` files (`pixel_generator.sv`, `input_builder.sv`, `mlp_pipeline.sv`, `mac_layer.sv`, `weight_ram.sv`, `packer.sv`) into something the block design can actually instantiate, i.e. a proper IP-Catalog entry. I open a throwaway Vivado project just for this, add the source files, and run `synth_design -rtl` first as a quick check — if I've broken a port somewhere or forgotten a module, I find out here in a few seconds instead of thirty minutes into a real synthesis run.

For the actual packaging I used the `ipx::` TCL commands (`package_project`, `merge_project_changes`, `infer_bus_interfaces`, `update_checksums`, `save_core`) instead of going through the IP Packager GUI. I tried the GUI route first and it kept spawning its own separate project state that didn't match what I expected, and re-doing it by hand every time I touched the source got old fast. Five TCL calls do the same job and I can just re-run the script. `infer_bus_interfaces` is the important one — it's what makes Vivado recognise my plain AXI-Stream and AXI-Lite ports as real bus interfaces, so I can wire them with `connect_bd_intf_net` in one go instead of dragging individual wires.

## patch_base_to_lean_nn.tcl

This is the script doing the real surgery, and it's the one I spent the most time on. `base.tcl` builds the stock design with HDMI-in going into one VDMA and HDMI-out coming from a second VDMA. My patch runs right after `base.tcl` and turns that into my single-direction setup: it deletes the old stream connection into the video block and rewires `pixel_generator_0/out_stream` straight into it, so the frame my MLP renders is what actually reaches the VDMA — not whatever the capture path was producing before. Then it deletes the second VDMA, the `hdmi_in` block, and the HDMI-in ports entirely, since I have no input video at all; the remaining VDMA's read side was already wired to HDMI-out by `base.tcl`, so I only needed to repoint its write side. I also deleted `proc_sys_reset_pixelclk`, but only after checking `base.tcl` directly to confirm it's wired exclusively to `hdmi_in`'s pixel clock and nothing on the output side — I didn't want to delete something load-bearing just because a comment said it was safe. Last thing it does is strip two debug trace-analyzer cells I'm not using, just to free up resources.

## build_lean_nn_bitstream.tcl

This one has no logic of its own, it's just there so I don't have to remember the right order. First thing it does is delete every stale Vivado-generated and cache directory (`./base`, `./build`, `./.Xil`, `./ip/.Xil`, `./ip/build`, the throwaway repackage project) before touching anything — I added this after Vivado reused a cached netlist from an old `pixel_generator` configuration on me once, so now "clean rebuild" actually means clean. Then it sources `repackage_pixel_generator.tcl`, `base.tcl`, and `patch_base_to_lean_nn.tcl` in order, runs synthesis and reports utilisation, runs implementation through to bitstream, and on success copies the `.bit`/`.hwh` pair out to fixed names so I always know where the latest build is.

## Why I needed all this

Without scripting it, going from "I changed `H1_SIZE`" to "working bitstream on the board" meant redoing a long sequence of GUI edits by hand every time, and any slip in that sequence — a missed reconnection, deleting things in the wrong order — tends to produce the exact same symptom (black screen, or registers silently reading zero) no matter which step I actually got wrong. Scripting it means every rebuild starts from the same known state and applies the same already-checked sequence of changes, and the v6 diagnostics in the patch script turn "did I just break the wiring" from a guess into something I can just read off the build log.
